**Pattern:** independent boutique operators (DataTrainers, BlueShore, Cegos) cluster at **$37–53 per participant-hour** for small (5–10 person), non-subsidized, in-house sessions. That band is the real benchmark for a new studio — NobleProg's headline number can't be cost-normalized because it withholds group size.

## 4. Can kadabra charge NobleProg range starting out?

Not yet, not on the full number, but the gap is smaller than it looks. NobleProg's $5,330/14h is a global franchise price list (same figure in Mexico, converted) sold via a 4,400-trainer subcontractor network with no CR-specific credibility (no reviews, no trainer bios found) — it's a brand premium, not a proven local benchmark. The real comparable is the boutique cluster (DataTrainers, BlueShore, Cegos) at $37–53/participant-hour for small in-house groups, unsubsidized. For a 1-day (6–8h) workshop with 8–12 people, that band implies roughly **$1,800–$4,200 per session** — below NobleProg's per-course sticker but well above CR's free/undisclosed public options (INA free, TEC/ia.co.cr/UCenfotec all opaque or subsidized), so there's real room above the local floor. A two-to-three person CR studio with zero track record should anchor near the **low-to-mid end of that band, ~$1,800–$2,800 for a 1-day, 8–12 person in-house session** (remote slightly under onsite), explicitly undercutting NobleProg's implied per-participant-hour while still beating "free" university/chamber alternatives on customization and speed. Move toward NobleProg-level pricing ($4,300–$5,330+) once kadabra has: 2–3 named repeat corporate clients, a published case study with a measurable outcome (hours saved, adoption rate), sector-specific content (banking/tourism/logistics), and bilingual on-site delivery with travel built in — the same things NobleProg substitutes with brand scale and trainer-network breadth. Certificates and post-workshop support (seen at DataTrainers, 100x.mx) are cheap to add and justify 10–15% above the base band immediately.
