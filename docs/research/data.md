## What's actually strong for Costa Rica — 10-line note

1. CR has real, sourced numbers, not just global borrowed stats: Procomer's 2025 TIC sector survey (86 companies) is a legitimate local dataset.
2. Headline strength: 76% of CR TIC firms already use gen AI/digital agents — adoption is not hypothetical here.
3. 93% report time savings on repetitive tasks — a believable, quotable "AI done right" stat with a CR name attached.
4. CR ranks #1 in Latin America for AI human talent/skills (ILIA 2025) — a genuine regional differentiator to lead with.
5. The Aura Interactiva "40x productivity" quote is vivid but is one firm's claim on "certain processes" — use as an anecdote, not a sector average.
6. The weak spot is real and useful for the failures half: only 9% of CR AI-adopting firms have formal governance, 12% have a dedicated AI team — adoption outpacing control is a homegrown, CR-specific version of "pilot purgatory."
7. CR-specific job-exposure data exists (19.2% of employment theoretically exposed, La Nación/MICITT-sourced) — good for the belief-vs-evidence puzzle, paired with the 94%-theoretical vs 33%-practical task-use gap.
8. No CR-specific company incident equivalent to Samsung/Deloitte/Air Canada was found — none of failure modes 1–2 have a confirmed local case; say so rather than invent one.
9. CEPAL/regional LatAm data (GDP-productivity link, 1.56% of global AI spend) situates CR within a real but data-thin region — don't overstate LatAm-wide figures as CR-specific.
10. Overall: CR's strongest honest pitch is "high adoption, high skills ranking, weak governance" — a credible reason a local AI-workshop company is needed, not just imported hype.
